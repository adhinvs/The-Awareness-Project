# Who You Are & What You Have

Your identity is made up of the personal details, digital traces, accounts, and assets connected to you. This includes information you share deliberately, as well as patterns created by your everyday online activity.

Together, these details reveal who you are, what you have, and how others can use that information. Even ordinary details can become valuable when they are collected, combined, and connected back to you.

## Myth Busters

**1. Personal data is not harmless just because it is public**<br />
Public details can be combined with private records to build a profile, target a person, or support impersonation.

**2. Targeted advertising and fraud use similar data trails**<br />
The same browsing patterns, location signals, and personal details can support both advertising and scams.

**3. Data collection is not the same as data control**<br />
Once information moves between apps, brokers, and services, it can be copied, combined, and resold beyond the place where you first shared it.

## The Story

Meera searched for a solo trip during a difficult week. Soon after, a time-limited trekking offer appeared that matched her mood and circumstances.

She had not told the platform she was going through a breakup. Her searches, scrolling patterns, location, and activity had already been combined into a profile that predicted what might persuade her.

The offer was not a coincidence. It was a calculation built from ordinary details she had left across different services.

## What the Experience Shows

The simulation shows how small details become valuable when they are combined and sold.

**The piece is harmless. The combination is not.**<br />

A name may seem harmless. Combined with a date of birth, location pattern, and emotional profile, it can support targeted fraud or manipulation.

**The ad market and the fraud market share the same infrastructure.**<br />

The same data pipelines can support targeted advertising, identity fraud, scam operations, and influence campaigns.

**You are not the customer. You are the inventory.**<br />

Free apps, loyalty cards, and personalised services often collect information about who you are, where you go, and what you do.

## Take Control: What Can You Do?

Pick one action to start with today.

**1. Check your exposure**<br />

Search your name and email online. Check [haveibeenpwned.com](https://haveibeenpwned.com) and review what your public profiles reveal.

**2. Reduce unnecessary access**<br />

Review app permissions for location, contacts, microphone, and camera. Remove access that an app does not need.

**3. Protect your email**<br />

Give your primary email a unique password and turn on two-factor authentication. It is the recovery key for almost everything else.
