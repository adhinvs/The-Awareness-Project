# Your Location & Physical Safety

Your location is not just where you are. It is connected to who you are, who you trust, and whether you are safe. A single place can reveal your home, workplace, relationships, routines, and the people around you.

Photos, apps, phone numbers, and daily routines can reveal where you live, where you go, and when you are most vulnerable. Sometimes this information is shared openly, and sometimes it is exposed through details you did not realise were visible.

## Myth Busters

**1. Location sharing is not limited to map apps**<br />
Photos, app permissions, Wi-Fi networks, phone signals, and posting patterns can all reveal where you are.

**2. A photo can reveal more than the subject**<br />
Reflections, landmarks, street signs, timestamps, and hidden metadata can expose a location even when no place is tagged.

**3. Physical safety and digital safety are connected**<br />
Location details can reveal routines, relationships, and moments when someone is away from home.

**4. The threat is not always a stranger**<br />
Former partners, family members, acquaintances, or followers may use location clues to monitor or approach someone.

## Statistical Evidence

Most people think of location sharing as something they consciously choose. In reality, your location leaks through dozens of channels you never deliberately opened.

## The Story

Maya was 23, living alone in a new city, and growing a modest following on Instagram.

She was careful, or so she thought. No address in her bio. No check-ins. No posts about her building.

One afternoon, she posted a close-up selfie from her apartment window. Good lighting. Natural background. Nothing suspicious.

Three weeks later, a man knocked on her door. He had found her using only her public photos.

He matched a faint reflection in the photo with satellite maps, then used older posts to confirm the neighbourhood. It took him a few hours and no hacking skills.

## What the Experience Shows

Location data can move quickly from a digital trace to a physical risk.

**1. Location is not a setting. It is a data exhaust.**<br />
Photos, posts, delivery orders, and app activity can reveal where you are. You do not need to share your location for it to be shared.

**2. The gap between digital exposure and physical danger is smaller than most people realise.**<br />
A stalker or scammer may need no hacking tool or password. Location information from photos, apps, routines, and phone numbers can be enough to find and follow someone.

**3. The people most at risk are often the least warned.**<br />
Location-based harm often comes from someone the victim already knows, such as a former partner, family member, or obsessive follower. Understanding location leaks is a personal safety skill.

**4. If something feels wrong, trust that instinct.**<br />
A familiar car in unfamiliar places or someone knowing details about your routine may be a warning. Document dates and times, and seek specialist support if you suspect stalking, stalkerware, or an unknown tracker.

## Take Control: What Can You Do?

:::caution[Check for location clues]

Photos can reveal more than GPS metadata. Review landmarks, reflections, street signs, and posting time before sharing images from a sensitive location.

:::


Pick one action to add to your routine.

**1. Clean photo location data**<br />

Turn off camera location tagging. Remove EXIF data before sharing, and post later when you are away from home.

**2. Review location permissions**<br />

Review Location Services on your phone. Change unnecessary "Always" access to "While Using" or "Never."

**3. Add a SIM lock**<br />

Ask your carrier for a SIM lock or port freeze. A separate PIN can help prevent someone from transferring your number.

**4. Remove public listings**<br />

Search your name on data broker sites and submit opt-out requests where your address appears. Recheck every six months.
