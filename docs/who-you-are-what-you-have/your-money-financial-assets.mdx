# Your Money & Financial Assets

Your financial life is spread across bank accounts, cards, wallets, apps, and investment platforms. Each one holds a different part of your money and depends on different passwords, devices, phone numbers, or recovery methods.

Each one has its own access points, risks, and rules about what happens when something goes wrong. A payment can be easy to make but difficult to reverse, especially when money moves through unfamiliar platforms or irreversible channels.

## Myth Busters

**1. A familiar payment method is not automatically reversible**<br />
UPI, bank transfers, crypto, and card payments follow different recovery rules. The method you choose affects what can happen next.

**2. A bank account has more than one door**<br />
Passwords, OTPs, recovery emails, SIM cards, and devices can all provide access. Protecting only the password leaves other routes open.

**3. Small test payments can be part of a larger scam**<br />
Fraudsters may allow a small withdrawal or refund to build trust before asking for a much larger transfer.

**4. Financial fraud can begin outside your bank account**<br />
Stolen identity details can be used to open loans, cards, wallets, or investment accounts in your name.

## The Story

David was careful with money. After a wrong-number text led to a trusted online contact, he tested a crypto investment platform with a small amount and withdrew staged profits.

He later moved $50,000 into it. When a withdrawal tax appeared, he paid. More fees followed, then the platform disappeared. The payment method offered no reliable reversal.

## What the Experience Shows

**1. Your financial life is spread across more access points than you realise.**<br />

A bank account may have several access points: password, OTP, recovery email, biometrics, or SIM. An attacker only needs one weak door.

**2. Cryptocurrency and blockchain assets have no safety net.**<br />

Crypto transactions are permanent once confirmed. Whoever controls the wallet keeps the funds. If a platform asks for crypto to unlock a withdrawal, stop.

**3. Most authorised transfers are not recoverable.**<br />

UPI and wire transfers are usually treated as authorised when you approve them, even if you were deceived. Pause before sending, because recovery is uncertain.

**4. Identity-based fraud can create debts in your name without your knowledge.**<br />

Identity documents can be used to pass KYC and open loans in your name. The debt may remain linked to you until you dispute it with evidence.

**5. Chargebacks exist, but only for credit cards.**<br />

Credit card charges can usually be disputed. Debit cards, UPI, wires, and crypto do not offer the same safety net. Report suspicious payments immediately.

## Take Control: What Can You Do?

:::danger[If money was just transferred]

Contact your bank or payment provider immediately through its official app or number. Report the transfer as soon as possible because recovery options can narrow with time.

:::


Pick one and do it this week.

**1. Map every access point**<br />

List every account, card, wallet, and investment platform. Check each recovery phone number, email, password, and PIN.

**2. Turn on alerts and limits**<br />

Enable instant transaction alerts and set daily transfer limits. You want to know within seconds if money moves unexpectedly.

**3. Pause before irreversible payments**<br />

Before a UPI, wire, or crypto payment, ask whether it can be reversed. If not, verify the recipient through a second channel.

**4. Check your credit report**<br />

Check your CIBIL, Experian, or CRIF report every few months. Report unfamiliar loans, cards, or enquiries through [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in), 1930, and the credit bureau.

**5. Add a fraud alert**<br />

Ask CIBIL, Experian, or CRIF for a fraud alert. If suspicious activity is already visible, ask about a credit freeze.

**6. Use safer payment methods**<br />

For unfamiliar online purchases, use a credit card when possible. Credit card charges can usually be disputed.
