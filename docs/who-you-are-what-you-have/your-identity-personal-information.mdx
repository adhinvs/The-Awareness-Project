# Your Identity & Personal Information

Your digital identity is the collection of personal details that represent you online, including your name, photo, phone number, email, and information connected to your accounts. These details appear across social media, forms, messages, purchases, and services you use every day.

Individually, these details may seem harmless. Together, they can help someone pretend to be you, access your accounts, or misuse your identity. The risk grows when separate pieces are combined into a profile that says more about you than you intended to reveal.

## Myth Busters

**1. Identity theft does not require every detail about you**<br />
An attacker may only need a few matching details to pass a check, create a convincing profile, or begin an account recovery attempt.

**2. A data breach does not stay limited to one website**<br />
Leaked details can be copied, combined with older records, and tested against other services.

**3. A fake profile can affect more than your reputation**<br />
Someone using your name or photo can mislead your contacts, damage your credibility, or create new opportunities for fraud.

## The Story

Neha was a teacher in her mid-thirties. She had a modest social media presence and was careful about what she shared.

One Monday morning, three colleagues messaged her asking if she was okay. She had no idea what they meant.

Someone had created a profile using her name, her photo from a public post, and her school's name from her bio. They had contacted her colleagues, her students' parents, and two of her closest friends - asking for urgent help and small transfers of money.

Neha had not been hacked. No one had broken into her accounts. Her phone was with her the entire time.

All it took was a name, a photo, a workplace, and a phone number. Everything was already visible.

It took her three weeks to have the fake accounts removed. Several people had already sent money. One parent never spoke to her again.

## What the Experience Shows

**Personal details become powerful when combined.**<br />
A name, photo, workplace, and phone number can be enough to build a convincing fake identity.

**Public information can become a private risk.**<br />
Details shared openly can be used to contact your friends, impersonate you, or target your accounts.

**Small privacy choices reduce the profile others can build.**<br />
Less public information means fewer pieces available for misuse.

## Take Control: What Can You Do?

:::caution[Small details can be enough]

A name, photo, workplace, and phone number can support a convincing impersonation. Review public profiles from the view a stranger would see.

:::


1. Search for yourself online. Check what your name, phone number, workplace, and email reveal publicly. Use [Have I Been Pwned](https://haveibeenpwned.com) to check for known breaches.
2. Lock your Aadhaar when it is not in use. Use [myAadhaar](https://myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in) to enable the biometric lock, then unlock it only when needed.
3. Reduce public profile details. Hide your phone number, address, workplace, and other details from people you do not know.
4. Protect your key accounts. Use unique passwords for email, social media, and your phone account. Turn on two-factor authentication.
5. Act quickly if something looks wrong. Report fake profiles, preserve evidence, contact [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in) or call 1930, and warn anyone who may be contacted in your name.
